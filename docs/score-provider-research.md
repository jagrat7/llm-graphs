## METR: why it wins the credibility slot

The current methodology describes private evaluation tasks, zero-data-retention requirements for inference on those tasks, scaffold tuning on a development set followed by a separate test set, six independent runs per task, and automatic/manual reward-hack screening followed by multiple human reviewers. METR discloses no AI-company funding or donations directed by frontier-company employees, while acknowledging significant free tokens and model access. These protections provide a stronger documented case against test contamination and superficial answer optimization than popularity alone.

Time Horizon 1.1 is chiefly software engineering, ML, and cybersecurity work. It measures a **model together with its agent scaffold**. A 50% horizon is the human-expert task duration at which the agent is predicted to succeed half the time: a task-difficulty measurement in minutes/hours. Keep this as a native capability metric; it is not AI execution duration or a percentage score. Confidence intervals and the 50%/80% reliability choice must remain attached to the estimate. The dashboard warns that horizons above 16 hours are unreliable, and says limited evaluation capacity leaves some recent models unmeasured.

The [January 2026 suite revision](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/) expanded the suite and corrected scoring/task problems. Its [modeling-sensitivity analysis](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-20-impact-of-modelling-assumptions-on-time-horizon-results/) exposes uncertainty from human baselines, curve fitting, and task selection. Protected tasks reduce gaming risk without removing dependence on the benchmark distribution or scaffold. **LiveBench is the alternative if broad capability coverage and recent-model coverage matter more than these protections.**

## Arena: why it wins the popularity slot

Popularity here means name recognition among AI-following users on X. Arena remains my choice for that role. Indexed X posts show its model rankings being quoted outside its own account, such as this [Gemini 3.1 Pro ranking discussion](https://x.com/AIMIRAI46487/status/2024693758978298145). This supports recognition but is not a representative survey or proof that it is the single most recognized benchmark. Artificial Analysis also meets the recognition threshold: this [independent account's discussion of its Muse Spark results](https://x.com/scaling01/status/2041914819545084223/photo/1) had about 74,500 views in the indexed snapshot. It remains the first slot rather than occupying two slots.

The current Text leaderboard's 8.6 million votes and 410 models establish participation and coverage, separately from X recognition. Its Sep 30 update establishes current results. Blind pairwise human voting and uncertainty estimates make it useful for comparing what people prefer. Preserve the leaderboard category, adjustment settings, snapshot date, and preliminary status; the score is a preference rating in native units.

Historical concerns about selective model variants and provider access remain relevant (see below). Arena has since published policy and ranking changes, and offers style-control and factuality adjustments. These warrant considering its current methods rather than treating the historical critique as a description of every current result. They do not establish immunity to stylistic optimization. Do not substitute Arena's displayed token price for the cost of the work producing a preference rating.

## Other candidates and evaluation merits

| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| LiveBench                | Peer-reviewed design; objective ground-truth scoring across several capabilities; question refreshes and temporarily withheld questions reduce contamination risk. | English-focused, limited open-ended quality coverage, prompt-family biases; industry sponsorship.                                                                     | My first choice for measured correctness across broad text capabilities.                                         |
| Artificial Analysis      | Current suite covers professional work, agents, coding, scientific reasoning, and general tasks; settings and index revisions are documented.                      | Mixes deterministic grading with LLM judges; some private datasets and evolving normalization limit independent reproduction.                                         | Stronger choice when the intended score emphasizes professional and agentic utility.                             |
| Epoch Capabilities Index | Fits a shared capability scale across over 50 benchmarks, accounts for differing difficulty and saturation, and reports bootstrap uncertainty.                     | Combines independent results with model-developer reports and inherits their selection/contamination biases; a latent estimate rather than one controlled evaluation. | Strong alternative for an aggregate capability estimate and progress over time.                                  |
| Arena                    | Blind crowdsourced comparisons capture user preferences on unconstrained prompts and report uncertainty.                                                           | Preference is not a direct correctness test; prompt sampling and presentation can influence results.                                                                  | Valuable as a separate preference score.                                                                         |
| Stanford HELM            | Strong prompt-level transparency, reproducibility, and academic provenance.                                                                                        | Some scenarios use LLM judges; framework entered maintenance mode in June 2026, so current evaluation coverage needs checking separately.                             | Strong methodology reference; no claim that its current leaderboard is the most representative frontier ranking. |

These judgments are based on the [LiveBench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19314v2), [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking), [Epoch methodology](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation/methodology) and [limitations](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation/faq), [Arena's ICML paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/chiang24b.html), and [HELM Capabilities methodology](https://crfm.stanford.edu/2025/03/20/helm-capabilities.html) and [current maintenance notice](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm). They are a reasoned selection for a stated measurement goal, not evidence of one universally best provider.

LiveBench's original paper discloses Abacus.AI sponsorship and both academic and industry affiliations. It should not be described as fully independent. Its refreshed questions reduce contamination risk without eliminating it, and objective scoring does not guarantee that its chosen tasks represent every kind of useful model behavior. The current score files have point estimates; the original paper reports bootstrap confidence intervals. These qualifications do not overturn the correctness-focused recommendation, but they matter when interpreting close ranks.

Epoch's [overview](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation) discloses Google DeepMind funding and collaboration on the underlying research, while identifying ECI as Epoch's own product. Its FAQ explicitly acknowledges potential cherry-picking in developer reports and benchmark optimization, and describes collecting independent evaluations to mitigate those problems. Breadth and statistical calibration are real strengths; they do not make every underlying observation independent.

Arena's [peer-reviewed 2025 critique](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/70a93f260a51123b3c0e33ecd1b4de97-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf) raised concerns about selective variant disclosure and asymmetric access. Those historical findings should be read alongside its [current publication policy](https://arena.ai/blog/policy) and [ranking changes](https://arena.ai/blog/ranking-method). Its [2026 factuality extension](https://arena.ai/blog/factuality-in-arena) adds an optional verified-factuality signal; it does not turn the default preference score into a universal correctness measure.

The implementation observations below are retained as reference after the quality assessment.

## Why LiveBench

LiveBench has a peer-reviewed [ICLR 2025 conference paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/e4a46394ba5378b3f9a186a5b4c650d1-Abstract-Conference.html). It evaluates answers against objective ground truth and refreshes its questions to reduce contamination. This gives a useful alternative to DeepSWE's engineering benchmark. The team describes the work as an [ICLR Spotlight](https://github.com/LiveBench/LiveBench). The [current leaderboard](https://livebench.ai/) covers 23 subtasks across seven categories: reasoning, coding, agentic coding, mathematics, data analysis, language, and instruction following.

- [Costs CSV](https://livebench.ai/cost_2026_06_25.csv): 66 rows with the exact same model-key set, subtask dollar totals, question counts, and precomputed cost-per-question columns.

The benchmark release is **2026-06-25**, the last entry in the official [release list](https://github.com/LiveBench/new-livebench/blob/main/src/lib/constants.js). The served files reported `Last-Modified: Tue, 29 Sep 2026 19:01:46 GMT`. The official [2026-09-29 commit](https://github.com/LiveBench/new-livebench/commit/caa4253c8a3aa93b5c7ec234e681d4f120a20240) added GPT-6.1 Sol xHigh alongside Max. The release date identifies the question set; it is not the latest model evaluation date. The older `livebench.github.io` page lags the current domain.

## Correct score and cost semantics

Raw subtask scores use **0–100**. Reproduce the official overall score: average subtasks within each category, then average the seven category scores equally. Do not average all 23 subtasks directly. The official [averaging implementation](https://github.com/LiveBench/new-livebench/blob/main/src/Table/Averaging.js) rounds the overall value to two decimals. Display it as “LiveBench 2026-06-25 overall”; it is a category-balanced benchmark score, not a pass rate on DeepSWE tasks.

Matching task cost **is available for the latest release**. Join score and cost by the identical raw model key and release. Use `sum(subtask dollar totals) / sum(subtask question counts)` across that release's scope, matching the official [cost computation](https://github.com/LiveBench/new-livebench/blob/main/src/lib/compute.js). The current CSV's `cost_per_question` agrees with that calculation to its four-decimal precision. `cost_per_successful_task` is a separate derived efficiency measure and should not populate our average task-cost metric.

The [data documentation](https://github.com/LiveBench/new-livebench#how-data-is-stored) says costs use recorded cache-aware `cost_usd` where available, otherwise evaluation token usage priced with official input/cached-input/output rates. These are published benchmark-run costs; they are not simply token list prices. Token prices remain separate metrics. Older releases may lack a cost file; missing or incomplete cost should remain unavailable. No wall-time or tokens-per-second measurement was found in these files.

## Integration fit and limits

Add a `livebench/` provider service exposing `score` and `costPerTask`, reusing the existing rule that task cost follows the score provider. Preserve the raw configuration key. Official [model metadata](https://github.com/LiveBench/new-livebench/blob/main/src/Table/modelLinks.js) identifies display name, organization, reasoning mode, and effort variants, including GPT-6.1 Sol Max/xHigh. The CSV also carries configurations such as `claude-opus-4-5-20251101-thinking-64k-high-effort`; preserve the date and thinking budget rather than silently merging incompatible configurations. Unknown effort stays unknown. Do not copy the leaderboard's default collapsing of effort variants; our graphs need separate rows.

Fetch all release files as one validated snapshot, use a release-aware cache key, and reject missing/nonfinite/out-of-range score cells rather than publishing a partial-category overall. Historical releases need extra care: the official averaging code hardcodes Grok-3 Thinking overall 72 and Grok-3 overall 58; a plain average would disagree with those official rows. Task-cost comparisons across providers represent different workloads; source notes should name the benchmark. Category-specific scores could follow later, but the current single-score interface can start with overall. LiveBench offers public versioned files rather than a documented stable data API, so retain parser fixtures and detect schema changes.

## Artificial Analysis implementation observations

The existing AA adapter already fetches `/api/v2/language/models/free`, but its transform discards evaluation/index-cost fields and its metric readers omit score. The [free API documentation](https://artificialanalysis.ai/data-api/docs) includes the headline Intelligence Index and benchmark `cost_per_task.total_cost`. Current methodology is v4.3.2; the API reports major/minor `4.3`. Keep native index units and version attribution, not a percent pass-rate label.

A live authenticated page-one check returned HTTP 200: 200 rows, 198 scored, 53 with numeric task cost, and four total pages. Examples: Gemini 3.5 Flash High score 32.6 / task cost $1.5625; MiMo V2.5 Pro score 26 / task cost $0.0541. Join score/cost from the same configuration and keep missing costs null. AA [cost methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking) uses billed evaluation-token usage plus typical cache rates and current pricing. These access details are not part of the evaluation-quality ranking above.
